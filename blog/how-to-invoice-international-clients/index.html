International Invoicing

How to Invoice International Clients Clearly

Create international invoices with an agreed currency, correct client details, clear tax treatment, payment instructions, and fee expectations.

By Invoice Next Editorial Team · Published 8 November 2026
Search intent: Learn how to invoice a client in another country.

Agree on the commercial details first

Before work starts, agree on the billing currency, price, payment schedule, external payment method, and who bears transfer or conversion fees. Put those details in the contract or proposal.

Use complete party information

Include the business names and addresses used for the transaction. Add registration or tax identifiers only when applicable and verify the destination country’s invoicing requirements.

Make currency unambiguous

Use the ISO currency code, such as USD, EUR, or GBP, next to totals. A dollar symbol alone may be ambiguous across countries. Avoid changing the invoice amount later because an exchange rate moved unless the agreement provides for it.

Handle taxes carefully

Cross-border tax treatment depends on the supplier, client, service or goods, and jurisdictions involved. Do not guess; confirm the applicable VAT, GST, sales-tax, withholding, or reverse-charge treatment with a qualified adviser.

Explain payment and fees

Provide accurate instructions for the agreed external payment channel, including any reference the client must use. State the agreed allocation of bank or platform fees.

Keep a complete record

Store the final invoice, relevant contract or estimate, proof of delivery where applicable, and payment record. Record the actual amount received and any separately recognized fee according to your accounting process.

Put the guide into practice

Use Invoice Next to create an invoice and keep the client, document, and payment record organized. Always review local legal and tax requirements.
